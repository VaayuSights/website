# Cold outreach

Three emails, one for each kind of prospect, plus the follow-up. All of them
make the same move the enquiry playbook rests on: **do not pitch, ask for a
sample.** Twenty frames costs them nothing to send and costs us an evening to
read, and it turns a cold email into a piece of work.

Keep them short. A cold email that runs past about 150 words does not get read,
and every sentence you add is a sentence that can be disagreed with.

---

## Before you send anything

**The domain is new.** vaayusights.in has almost no sending history, so a burst
of two hundred emails will land in spam and damage the domain's reputation
permanently — including for the mail you actually care about. Start at **ten to
fifteen a day** for the first fortnight, then raise it slowly. SPF, DKIM and
DMARC are already in place, which is the hard part.

**Send from `pranaam@vaayusights.in`**, not from the `shri@` mailbox. The alias
is the published address and it does not carry a first name.

**Plain text. No tracking pixels, no images, no link shorteners.** All three hurt
deliverability and none of them help at this volume. One plain link to the site
is fine.

**One real line of personalisation each.** Name the site, the asset, the state,
the recent project — something that proves the email was not blasted. Without
it these read as templates, because they are.

**Never attach anything** to a first email.

## 4. Analysis support — inspection and analytics companies

A different kind of prospect: organisations that already capture aerial data and
already have analysts. The pitch is capacity behind their team, not analysis
instead of it. Deliberately says nothing about how the recipient runs their
operation — a two-week-old practice telling a funded company where its
bottlenecks are reads badly, and the generic version works on everyone.

**Subject:** `Analysis support — VaayuSights, Pune`

> Hello [ name ],
>
> I am writing to introduce VaayuSights, an aerial-data analysis practice based
> in Pune, India. We provide analysis support to organisations working with
> aerial inspection data.
>
> What we take on:
>
> Defect review and classification to an agreed taxonomy. Quality assurance and
> second-pass audit. Annotation for training sets — boxes, polygons, masks and
> classification. Report production to an agreed template. We work in thermal
> and RGB imagery across blades, panels and structures, and every batch is
> reviewed by an engineer before it returns.
>
> Analysis is entirely in-house. We do not operate a drone fleet, so we are not
> in competition with any capture operation.
>
> If it would be useful to see the quality rather than discuss it: send us ten
> turbines and we will return the completed analysis within two working days,
> at no cost.
>
> That turnaround is possible because detection runs through our own pipeline
> rather than frame by frame. Every finding is still reviewed by an engineer
> before it leaves us — the speed is in the first pass, not in the judgement.
>
> We work under NDA, do not subcontract, and delete source imagery on
> completion.
>
> If this is not relevant, a one-line reply saying so is genuinely useful and I
> will not follow up.
>
> Regards,
> VaayuSights
> Aerial-data analysis · Pune, India
> pranaam@vaayusights.in · vaayusights.in

**Send it to Operations or Engineering, never sales.** Director of Operations,
Head of Inspection Services, Head of Delivery, or an engineering manager for
perception. Sales files a vendor email under vendor.

**The paragraph about the pipeline is not optional.** Ten turbines is one to
four thousand images; anyone technical will work out that it cannot be done by
hand in two days. Explaining that first-pass detection is automated and
judgement is human turns an implausible claim into the most interesting line in
the email. Only send it while that is true — if the pipeline needs hand-holding,
offer three turbines in five working days instead. A kept small promise beats a
missed large one, and a pilot is the only evidence of competence a cold prospect
will ever have.

## 5. The follow-up

Send once, five working days later, on the same thread. Most replies come from
this one, and most people never send it. Send it once and then stop.

Works for all four emails above. **Subject:** leave it as `Re: <the original subject>`

> Short follow-up on the below.
>
> If the timing is wrong or it is not for you, say so and I will not write
> again.
>
> If you do have survey data sitting unused, the offer stands: 20–30 frames,
> two working days, no charge.

That opt-out line is not politeness. It raises replies, and a clean "not for
us" is worth more than silence — it tells you to stop spending attention there.

---

## What not to write

- **No metrics.** No recovered-yield percentages, no defect counts, no "we have
  analysed X MW". There is no past work to cite yet and an invented figure is
  the one mistake that cannot be walked back.
- **No fleet.** Capture is partner-run. If asked directly, the answer is in
  `copy.md` section 6.
- **No price.** Not in the first email, not in the second. Pricing happens after
  seeing data, which is the whole point of the sample.
- **No "I hope this email finds you well."** Open on their problem.
- **No personal name in the sign-off.** Sign as VaayuSights.

## Target list — who to approach

Built 13 Nov from search results only. **Nothing here was verified by reading a
company's own site** — the sandbox had no outbound network access that day — and
every "largest", "leading" or "India's top" claim below is the company's own
marketing, not an independent finding. Check each one before writing to it.

### First, the thing that changes the pitch

India already has a drone-data-processing outsourcing industry, and it sells to
exactly the overseas clients VaayuSights would target. MAPe IT Solutions
(Chennai) states outright that it provides drone data processing outsourcing to
clients in the USA, UK, Middle East and Australia. Geodraft India (Karnataka)
lists photogrammetry, LiDAR processing and classification. Goodland Surveys
(Chennai), AIRPIX and Spatem GeoTeck all sell processing as a service.

These are competitors, not prospects. It means "Indian firm, cheaper, overnight
turnaround" is not a differentiator — several firms already say it, some for
nearly a decade. What is left to compete on is the engineer-reviewed second pass
and defect judgement rather than bulk photogrammetry output. Keep the pitch on
analysis and judgement, not on processing volume or price.

### Tier 1 — Indian drone operators (use section 2)

The warmest prospects, and the right place to start. Same time zone, no trust
gap from being offshore, and operators genuinely tend to lack processing staff.
A two-week-old practice can get a reply here in the same week.

Skip **ideaForge** for this pitch. They are a UAV manufacturer, not a survey
services business, so there is no analysis queue to take work from.

### Tier 2 — global inspection and analytics firms (use section 4)

Higher value, much harder, and slower. Expect procurement, NDAs and a pilot
before anything moves. Worth writing to a few, but not before Tier 1 has
produced a delivered job to point at.

Best-fit first, by how their own words describe a human analysis step:

1. **Applus+** — describes AI-collected data being "analysed and validated by
   technicians who are blade inspection experts". That is precisely the
   second-pass work this practice sells. The closest fit found.
2. **Sulzer & Schmid** — rotor blade inspection, built on detailed visual
   analysis of blade condition. Human-judgement heavy.
3. **Above Surveying** — Essex, UK, founded 2016, serves solar asset owners
   worldwide through its SolarGain processing application. Real analysis volume.
4. **Zeitview** — already drafted in section 4, not yet sent.
5. **Cyberhawk** — high-value asset inspection, tailored service models.
6. **Nearthlab**, **Perceptual Robotics**, **vHive**, **SkyVisor**,
   **Percepto**, **Clobotics**, **Delair**, **Sentera** — platform-led, so the
   analysis is largely their product. Lower priority.

Deprioritise **Raptor Maps** and **Sitemark**. Both are software companies whose
automated detection *is* the product; Raptor Maps coordinates capture through a
network of drone service providers rather than employing analysts. There is
little human analysis to support.

### Sequencing

Tier 1 until one job has been delivered. Then Tier 2, with that job as the only
evidence a cold prospect will ever care about. Section 4's ten-turbine offer is
the hook for Tier 2; section 2's 20–30 frames is the hook for Tier 1.

Do not start either at volume before the rate card exists. A reply asking "what
do you charge" with no answer ready wastes the one opening that outreach buys.
