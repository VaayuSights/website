# Handling an enquiry

What to do when a lead lands. Written for the stage we are at: no case
studies, no client list, and a genuine advantage in being faster and
straighter than a large consultancy.

The whole approach rests on one move — **do not quote, ask for a sample**.
It qualifies the client, proves we can do the work without a portfolio, and
surfaces problems with their data while that is still helpful rather than
embarrassing.

---

## The sequence

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acknowledge | within hours |
| 2 | Qualify | same day |
| 3 | Ask for a sample | same day |
| 4 | Deliver the sample analysis | 48 hours, bounded |
| 5 | Quote | only after seeing data |
| 6 | Ask about an anonymised case study | when it goes ahead |

---

## 1. Acknowledge — within hours

The site promises one business day. Beat it. Speed is one of the few
advantages a one-person practice has over a firm with a sales process.

> Thanks for getting in touch — this has reached the right place.
>
> I'll come back properly later today with a few questions so I can give you
> a straight answer rather than a generic one.

---

## 2. Qualify — five questions

Ask these before anything else. Question 3 decides everything.

```
1. What asset, and roughly what scale?
   (e.g. 12 MW solar farm, 40 turbines, 400 m bridge)

2. What are you trying to find, or what decision does this feed?

3. Do you already have imagery? Who flew it, when, which sensor,
   and was it flown for inspection or for mapping?

4. Is there a deadline driving this?

5. Who else needs to sign this off?
```

**On question 3.** Inspection photos and photogrammetry surveys are not
interchangeable. A set of close-ups will find defects and will never
reconstruct into a model. If they want a digital twin from inspection
imagery, say so immediately and offer to specify the re-fly. That
conversation early reads as expertise; the same conversation after invoicing
reads as excuse-making.

---

## 3. Ask for a sample — the move that matters

> Before I quote anything, send me 20–30 frames from the survey.
>
> I'll run them and come back with what that dataset can and cannot give
> you — at no cost. If the capture does not support what you are after, I
> would rather tell you now than after you have paid for a full run.

Why it works:

- costs an evening, not a project
- demonstrates capability where we have no portfolio to point at
- a client who sends data is a client who is serious
- it finds the data problems while they are still fixable

---

## 4. Deliver the sample — small, real, bounded

A short PDF, two or three pages:

- what we found, with two or three annotated frames
- what the full dataset would add
- anything about the capture that limits the result
- what a full engagement would cover

**Bound it explicitly.** One sample, limited scope, 48 hours. Free analysis
expands to fill whatever space it is given, and an unbounded favour for a
client who never converts is the most expensive thing a new practice can do.

---

## 5. Quote — only now

With the dataset seen, the quote is informed rather than hedged. Price the
work, not the hope.

State plainly what is included, what is not, and what the client provides.
If capture is needed, say that it runs through a partner operator we scope
and brief — that answer is in `copy.md` section 6 and it holds up better
than implying a fleet.

---

## 6. Ask about the case study

When it goes ahead:

> If this proceeds, may we reference it later as an anonymised case study —
> no client name, no location, no identifying detail?

This matters more than it looks. The site currently runs on stock
photography and honestly-framed capability claims. The first real defect
map, point cloud or thermal analysis replaces all of that, and nothing
generated can substitute for it.

Most clients say yes when the request is anonymous and asked at the start
rather than after delivery.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not quote from the enquiry alone.** Without seeing data the number is
  a guess, and a guess is either too high to win or too low to deliver.
- **Do not claim a fleet.** Capture is partner-run. The honest version is
  stronger and survives the second question.
- **Do not invent a result.** No percentages, no recovered-yield figures, no
  defect counts from work we have not done.
- **Do not let the free sample grow.** One dataset, one short document, a
  stated deadline.
- **Do not go quiet.** If an answer will take days, say so. Silence loses
  more early deals than a bad quote.
