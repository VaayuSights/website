**Posting notes**
- Post as the page. Do **not** reshare from a personal profile: that is what
  would attach a name to the company, and the page is anonymous by choice.
  It costs reach — a new page normally spreads through the founder's own
  network — which is exactly why the next two bullets matter more here than
  they would for most launches.
- Reply to every comment in the first two hours, as the page. That window
  decides whether LinkedIn keeps showing the post.
- No links in the post body; LinkedIn suppresses reach on those. Put the
  website in the first comment, posted as the page, straight after publishing.
- Sending the page link privately to contacts is fine and is the main lever
  left. Be aware that a contact replying "congratulations <name>" in the
  comments undoes the anonymity in one line, so ask them to engage with the
  page rather than with you.
- Plain text only. Avoid the bold-unicode trick in the body: screen readers
  read those characters as gibberish or skip them. The two bold lines kept
  below are a deliberate exception for scanning; drop them if you would rather
  the post stayed fully accessible.

### Post 2 — the question nobody asks until it is too late

Image: `social/posts/post2-overlap.png`


> "Can you build us a 3D model from this?"
>
> Sometimes the honest answer is no — and it is worth knowing why before you
> commission a survey.
>
> A 3D reconstruction is not built from photographs of a thing. It is built
> from photographs that overlap each other by 70 to 80 percent, flown in a
> deliberate pattern, with oblique passes for anything vertical.
>
> Inspection photos are different. A few close-ups of a cracked panel are
> perfect for defect detection and useless for reconstruction. There is
> nothing wrong with the images. They were flown for a different question.
>
> So if a 3D model is on your list, decide that before the drone goes up, not
> after. The flight plan changes.
>
> Send us a sample from any survey and we will tell you straight what it can
> and cannot produce. No charge for that answer.
>
> #DroneSurvey #Photogrammetry #DigitalTwin #AssetManagement

### Post 3 — thermal

Image: `social/posts/post3-thermal.png`


> A solar panel that looks perfect can be costing you money.
>
> Visible-light imagery shows you what is broken. Thermal shows you what is
> failing.
>
> A cell with a bad solder joint, a bypass diode that has given up, a string
> that has quietly disconnected — none of it leaves a mark you can photograph.
> It leaves heat.
>
> That is the whole case for radiometric thermal: it finds the faults that
> have not become visible damage yet, while they are still cheap to fix.
>
> The catch is that a thermal image is only as good as the conditions it was
> flown in. Irradiance, time of day, panel temperature, wind — get those wrong
> and you get a pretty picture with no diagnostic value.
>
> If you already fly thermal, send us a set. We will tell you whether the
> conditions support a real fault analysis.
>
> #ThermalImaging #SolarEnergy #PredictiveMaintenance #OandM

## 6. The question you will get asked

**"Do you fly it yourselves?"**

Answer it straight, because the site already does:

> Capture runs through vetted partner operators we scope and brief to our own
> spec, so the data arrives fit to analyse. The analysis is entirely in-house,
> and our cleaning crews are our own.

That is a stronger answer than claiming a fleet. It says you control the spec
and own the part that matters, and it will not unravel on the second question.
